Amiro Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-25

This policy describes what Amiro ("the App") does with your information. Amiro is local-first: your identity, avatar, and passport (people you've met) live on your device by default. There is no email-and-password Amiro account, and no third-party analytics or advertising SDK in the App. The App does use the network for (1) optional profile backup/restore, (2) verifying real-money Store purchases, and (3) optional, consent-gated diagnostics — see those sections below. Sharing with another person (QR/NFC) still goes directly device-to-device and does not pass through our servers.

Who this covers

Amiro is published by Developer's Coffee. This policy applies to the Amiro mobile app on Android and iOS. It does not cover any third-party app, website, or service you reach by tapping a link inside Amiro (for example, a link you added to your own profile).

What information the App handles

When you set up your Amiro identity, you may enter:

  • Display name and username
  • A short bio
  • Mobile number, email address
  • Social handles (X/Twitter, Instagram) and a personal website URL

This is stored on your device in a local database. Your avatar (body, hair, outfit, and other cosmetic choices) is stored the same way.

Optional profile backup (Supabase)

To help you recover your identity after reinstalling or moving to a new phone, the App can back up your profile to a Supabase database we operate:

  • Off until you turn it on. Identity settings show a cloud-backup switch. Turning it on asks you to confirm. The App does not upload a profile on first launch just because you opened it. After you accept, it creates an anonymous Supabase session and uploads your id, display name, username, avatar gender, avatar definition JSON, and privacy flags. Contact fields are included only when you marked them public. Row access is protected by Supabase row-level security; the App uses Supabase's public ("anon") key, not a service-role key.
  • The App gives you a recovery code (stored on your device) you can use on another install to pull that profile back. The code is not a password for a Developer's Coffee account.
  • The backup may also store RevenueCat's anonymous purchase user id next to your profile so purchases and identity can be reconciled after restore — not your name, email, or phone from the profile form.
  • Your passport / encounter history stays local only (not uploaded).
  • Turning backup off deletes that profile row when this device's anonymous session is the owner that created it, then clears the recovery code and session stored on the device. It also deletes web-profile rows for that same Supabase user. If this device is not the owner session, or the copy was already removed, the App does not pretend the server copy is gone. It shows an error. Email coffee.devloper@gmail.com to request deletion. There is no recovery-code server delete.
  • Some installs from before the opt-in switch may already have a server row while the switch shows off. Turning backup on and then off again is the in-app path that issues the owner delete.

If backup fails (no network), the App keeps working from local storage only.

Sharing with another person

Amiro's core feature is sharing your profile with someone else, by:

  • Showing them a QR code, or scanning theirs
  • Tapping phones together (NFC), on Android

Before you share, you choose which fields are public. Only the fields you mark public are included in what you show or send — anything you leave private is not included in the QR/NFC payload. The other person's phone reads that information directly from the QR code or NFC tap; it does not pass through Developer's Coffee servers.

If you scan someone else's QR code or receive their NFC tap, whatever public profile information they chose to share is stored on your device, the same way your own profile is. You control that data (including deleting it) on your device.

Camera and NFC

  • The App requests camera access only to scan a QR code someone shows you. No photo or video is captured, stored, or transmitted — the camera feed is used solely to detect a QR code.
  • The App requests NFC access only to read an incoming tap-to-share, and (Android only) to let your phone emulate a tag so someone else can read your shared profile via NFC. No other NFC use.

Purchases

The Store lets you buy optional cosmetic items with real money through Google Play Billing. Purchases are processed by RevenueCat, which we use to verify a purchase actually happened before unlocking the item.

What that involves:

  • The App SDK sends RevenueCat a random per-install identifier (not your name, email, or anything from your Amiro profile) and the product you're buying. RevenueCat validates the purchase with Google Play and reports the result back to the App.
  • RevenueCat notifies our backend (a Cloudflare Worker) when a purchase completes. We record the event — the random per-install identifier, the product bought, and the timestamp — in a Supabase database, so we can look up and restore your purchases if needed (for example, after reinstalling). This record never includes your name, email, phone number, or any other profile field from the identity form.
  • None of this is used for advertising, and we don't sell or share it with anyone beyond RevenueCat and Google Play, who process it to complete the purchase.

If you don't use the Store's paid items, none of the purchase network traffic applies to you — free cosmetics unlock without a purchase request.

Optional crash reports

Some release builds can send error summaries to help us fix bugs:

  • Crashes are always logged locally on your device first.
  • Upload is off by default. In Identity settings you can turn on Share crash reports to send scrubbed error text (message and stack) to our Cloudflare Worker, which stores it in Supabase for engineering review.
  • Only a short message (error type plus scrubbed summary), allowlisted Dart package: / dart: stack frames, app version, and platform are uploaded. Secrets such as JWTs, bearer tokens, UUIDs, emails, phone numbers, and file paths are stripped on device and again on the server. Your profile, passport, and purchase data are not included.
  • Cloudflare may process your IP address in transit for DDoS protection and rate limiting; we do not store IP in the crash database.
  • Server copies are deleted automatically after 90 days.
  • Turn the setting off any time — uploads stop immediately. Uninstalling removes the local log. See docs/protocols/crash-report-data.md.

Optional product analytics

You can help us understand whether setup, sharing, and the Store are working:

  • Off by default. In Identity settings, Privacy & diagnostics → Product analytics sends a small set of anonymous counts (for example, setup finished, first share, purchase completed).
  • Events include only coarse day, app version, platform, and a random install-scoped id we regenerate when you turn analytics off.
  • No names, contacts, avatar data, encounter details, share links, or prices.
  • Data goes to our Cloudflare Worker → Supabase with server-side allow-lists. We do not store your IP or browser User-Agent with analytics rows.

What the App does not do

  • No traditional account (no email sign-up, no password login).
  • No third-party analytics or advertising SDKs.
  • No selling of your data.
  • No location data is collected.
  • Passport data is not uploaded to our servers.
  • Opt-in web profile (https://id.amiro.app/u/…): only if you publish from Share settings (hidden in store builds until explicitly enabled). Published name, public contact fields, avatar PNG, and optional cosmetic summary are stored in Supabase and served by our Cloudflare Worker. Revoking wipes those fields server-side and the link returns gone (410).
  • Other sharing (QR/NFC amiro://encounter) stays device-to-device unless you choose the HTTPS link on QR/NFC after publishing.
  • Optional profile backup and purchase audit still use the network as described above.

Your control over your data

  • Editing or clearing a field in your identity removes it on your device immediately; the next successful backup sync updates the cloud copy.
  • Uninstalling the App removes local Amiro data, subject to your device's own OS behavior (for example, an OS-level backup you created yourself).
  • There is no password-based Amiro account to delete.
  • Deleted when an owner device turns backup off successfully: the profile row for that identity, and that user's web-profile rows. The recovery code and anonymous session are cleared on the device.
  • Kept: the anonymous Supabase Auth user is not deleted by that toggle. Purchase audit rows are not deleted by turning backup off. Crash and analytics rows, when you opted in, follow their own 90-day retention.
  • To remove a leftover cloud profile, an anonymous auth user, or a purchase audit row, email coffee.devloper@gmail.com. These are not always removed on uninstall because they exist to support restore. QR/NFC fields are described in the encounter-share protocol. Bundled fonts and meshes are listed in the repository license file.

Children's privacy

Amiro is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from children. We do not ask for age. Profile backup and purchases are initiated by whoever uses the App on the device.
